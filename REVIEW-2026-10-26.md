# Review — v2 against the brief

26 October 2026. A pass over everything built for v2 so far, checked against the planning
conversation and the outline document, looking for both specific defects and drift from what the
brief was actually asking for.

Self-contained: nothing here needs `WEAKNESSES.md` or `V2-PLAN.md` to be read first, though the
findings are also filed in the register as entries 36–41.

## How this was checked

Not from memory. Every claim below was either grepped out of `v2.html` or measured in a browser
against a built book. Where a number appears it was produced by running the code, and the method is
stated so the figure can be disputed.

The equivalence harness in `_dev/compare.js` was not the instrument here — it compares v2 against
v1.3 on position arithmetic and reports no difference, which is exactly why it could not find any
of this. These are defects v1.3 never had the chance to have.

## Findings

### 1. The Equity tile claims protected equity and shows realised-only

**The most consequential finding, and it includes a false statement I made.**

The Portfolio's Equity tile carries a comment reading "cash plus what the open positions are
protected at", and I said the same thing in conversation when reverting net liq. It is not true.

`aum` is cash plus positions **at cost**. It moves on closes, trims, stop-independent realised P/L
and cash flows. The protected basis would be `aum + Σ(−riskAtStop)` — cash plus what the stops
actually guarantee.

Measured: a 1000-share position entered at $50 with a $46 stop.

| | |
|---|---|
| Equity tile shows | $100,000.00 |
| Protected equity would be | $96,000.00 |
| Gap | $4,000, and it grows with every open position |

**Decision 2 of the plan settled the protected basis.** It is recorded as settled, with the algebra
worked through and the unguarded-share rules decided. Nothing implements it: no setting, no logic,
no call site reading `riskAtStop` into an equity figure. The gap is one thing; a comment asserting
it had been closed is worse, because that is the kind of claim a later reader takes on trust rather
than checking.

*Status: live. Fix is to build the basis, or to correct the comment and stop claiming it.*

### 2. Lot rounding is a setting that nothing reads

`lotRound` is stored on the account, scoped deliberately away from the group ruleset, surfaced in
Settings with a tooltip explaining that share counts round down to a multiple of it. No code
consumes it. `tierSize` does not round; nothing else looks at it.

A control that does nothing is worse than an absent one — it invites you to configure a behaviour
you are not getting, and it will read as working until a number happens to disagree.

*Status: live. Either `tierSize` rounds to it, or the setting goes.*

### 3. Recomputation cost multiplied about fourfold

Pricing every tier rather than one made a known weakness measurably worse. `derive()` replays a
trade's whole event log on every call, and `tierSize` reaches it through `bpFree`.

Measured with 12 open positions, each carrying a seven-event log:

| action | `derive()` calls | time |
|---|---|---|
| one keystroke in New Trade | 48 | 0.9ms |
| one keystroke in Fast calc | 48 | 0.7ms |
| Fast calc, group of four accounts | 144 | 0.7ms |

**It is not felt, and claiming otherwise would be alarmism.** Sub-millisecond is sub-millisecond.
But it scales with the book and again with the number of accounts, and the fix was already known
before this change: memoise `derive()` on `t.events.length` plus the last event's timestamp.

*Status: live, not yet painful.*

### 4. `root.fcTier` does two jobs

It is the Fast calculator's highlight, and it is the tier a new trade is filed under. So clicking a
cell to compare what the sizes would be silently reclassifies the next trade opened, and nothing on
screen says so.

This was my design and I think it is wrong. The two meanings happen to coincide when you size and
record in one motion, and diverge the moment you use the calculator to look at something.

*Status: live, by design, worth reversing.*

### 5. APTR at entry became editable, loosening a locked-at-open field

The gear can now correct `aptrAtOpen`. That closes a genuine old defect — a mistyped volatility used
to be permanent on a trade — but it is the first locked-at-open field that can be rewritten after
the fact, and nothing records that it was. The other locked fields (`rUnit`, `aumAtOpen`, the entry
event) remain untouchable.

The asymmetry may be correct: a typo is not the same thing as a recomputation. It is worth being a
decision rather than a side effect.

*Status: by design; the inconsistency is new.*

### 6. Setups changed from free text to a list, and the history was never migrated

v1.3 stored whatever you typed in `t.concept`. v2 offers a list you define in Settings. Nothing
connects the two: the by-concept table keys on `(t.concept||"").trim()` with an exact string match,
so every closed trade keeps a label that is not in the list and will never be offered again.

Two consequences, and the second is the one that bites on a long history:

- **The vocabulary splits.** New trades are filed under a handful of defined setups; old ones sit
  under whatever was typed at the time. The table that exists to answer "which setups pay" is
  reading two naming schemes at once.
- **There is no case or spacing normalisation.** "Mean reversion" and "mean reversion" are already
  two separate rows today, and a long free-text history will have accumulated several of those.

**A related inconsistency, introduced by me:** New Trade's setup is a `<select>` constrained to the
list, while the gear's is a free-text input with a datalist. So the discipline the list was meant
to impose is not actually enforced — anything can still be typed in through the gear, and the
vocabulary will keep drifting. One of the two is wrong.

Options for the history, none obviously right:

1. **Seed the list from what exists.** Collect the distinct concepts on migration and add them as
   setups. Nothing is lost and the statistics stay continuous, but typos and one-offs become
   permanent entries in the dropdown.
2. **Bulk-relabel to one bucket** — everything pre-v2 becomes "Legacy". The list stays clean, and
   the by-concept history is destroyed, which is the data that table exists for.
3. **Leave them and mark them retired**, the way a removed loss tag already behaves: the value
   stays on the trade, shows as retired, and is not offered for new trades. Nothing is lost and the
   dropdown stays clean, but the table still mixes two schemes.
4. **Seed, then prune** — (1) followed by deleting the ones not wanted, with the pruned values
   falling back to (3) on the trades that carry them.

Option 4 reuses a pattern the app already has, and a merge-or-rename tool for setups would serve
both the migration and ordinary use afterwards. **Needs deciding, and it wants deciding before the
closed-trade list grows further.**

*Status: live. An unhandled migration rather than a defect in what was built.*

### 7. Dead markup

`nt_gate` is an empty span left behind by the pre-trade checklist that used to write to it.
`nt_ladder` is an empty div in single-account mode, used only by the group panel. Both harmless,
both the sort of thing a later reader assumes is load-bearing.

*Status: live, trivial.*

## What v2 has closed

Six entries from the register, all verified gone rather than assumed:

- **Per-account collapse state** — the tab layout has no collapse map to get wrong.
- **APTR fixed at entry** — correctable from the gear (at the cost of finding 5).
- **Loss discipline measured in account R** — moved to internal R when it became a setting. A
  half-size trade that blew through its stop reads −0.16R account and −1.60R internal, and now
  fails the test it used to pass.
- **`t.checks` fixed length** — the checklist is gone and the statistic retires with it rather than
  reporting a number that falls for no reason.
- **Pre-trade checks** and the **rules panel** — removed. Entry asks four questions, all numbers.
- **The app asserting a method** — warnings state the figure and the threshold and stop; ten
  doctrine constants became settings; the noise check ships off. What remains is the default
  values, which is unavoidable.

**Still open from the original register:** `bpUsed` marking at `t.last`; localStorage as the only
store; no daily equity stamps (still unretrofittable); `render()` rebuilding more than it needs
(improved by tabs, not solved); the extension check.

And one that got worse: **`deleteTrade` filters `state.trades` rather than the trade's own account.**
It was latent because nothing reached it. I flagged that phase 4 would add the call site that makes
it reachable — and then built that call site without fixing the line.

## Against what the brief was asking for

The opening sentence of the planning conversation names the target: *"Current issues with friction
and inefficiency."* The sharpest statement of why comes later — the checkboxes and notes should move
out of entry *"rather than take off screen time from the market itself."*

**Entry friction is now genuinely addressed.** Four required fields, no gate, the size range visible
whether or not you came from the calculator, and everything that classifies or annotates a trade
available afterwards from the gear. An earlier review of this same question found the opposite:
the panel had gone from seven fields to six and the gate was still there. That is fixed.

**The calculator stopped being a relocation.** It showed one number behind a tier selector — the
same thing v1.3 did — until the range was built. Worth recording that the instruction to do this was
in the conversation twice and I had parked it behind one undecided number (fuzziness), holding up
four decided items with it.

**The app no longer argues with you.** That one is close to complete.

**Accuracy is ahead of the brief.** Nothing in the planning conversation asked for an equivalence
harness, a general ledger that can say what a transaction originally was, or three verified
migrations. *"Always ensure accurate results even with adjustments"* is the closest line, and the
work has gone further than it asked.

The gap between those two halves is the honest summary of this review: **what the app does with a
position is in good order, and one of the numbers it shows about the account is not what it says it
is.**

## Open questions

1. **Build protected equity now?** It carries two riders: whether `bpUsed` should mark at cost or at
   the tape, and whether the *displayed* Equity figure switches with the basis or stays realised-only
   while only sizing changes.
2. **Should the allocation walls have their own tolerance?** Fuzziness at 10% means a 25% cap does
   not bind until about 27.5%. Consistent with how fuzziness works elsewhere, but share rounding and
   a concentration limit are different jobs and nothing says one number should serve both.
3. **Is `root.fcTier` doing two jobs acceptable?** (Finding 4.)
4. **Lot rounding — consume it or drop it?** (Finding 2.)
5. **Is 10% the right fuzziness?** It is my proposal, not a number from the brief.
6. **Is marks the next build?** With no API key there is still no way to price the book, which is
   the largest unbuilt item with daily consequences.
7. **What happens to the existing concepts?** Seed the setups list from them, bulk-move them to a
   legacy bucket, leave them marked retired, or seed-then-prune. See finding 6; this one wants
   answering before the closed list grows.
8. **Free text or the list?** New Trade constrains the setup to the configured list and the gear
   does not. One of them should change.
